The Human in Computing and Cognition Lab has principles that guide the work we do and the pathways we work to create and understand. They are centered on multi-level understanding to critically create technological systems that _meet the moment)_. Here, by critical we follow theory and a praxis that is grounded in perspectives found in Black Radical Traditions (which include Black Feminist perspectives). These principles are living as we don't expect to be able to meet our moment unless we continue to learn and adapt.

## Principle 1: Cognition is key
We seek to ensure we use principled (where available) explanations for individuals grounded in theoretical perspectives from computational cognitive science.

## Principle 2: Multi-level perspectives
While we do tend to center individual (micro) level explanation towards understanding the ways people think about and interact with computing systems, we do this with an understanding of the importance of knowledge, actions, etc. at the meso (community/organization) and macro (institution) levels. We particularly think through how [_genres of the human_](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/51630) impact individuals and communities, and how we might model this is on multiple levels.

## Principle 3: Meeting the moment
THiCC Lab seeks to meet the needs of the current moment with the work we do, continually working to ensure we are not leaving the present while looking back at the past and towards a better future.

## Princple 4: Foundations in the Black Radical Tradition
THiCC Lab builds upon a foundation that is laid by [Black radical tradition](https://daily.jstor.org/cedric-robinson-the-black-radical-tradition/), which includes Black feministic thought and work. 

## Principle 5: Liberatory pathways
We have the understanding that as we create pathways towards more liberatory futures we must not _universalise_ such that we are developing for one [_universal standard genre of the human_](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9754990/). Instead, we hope to create pathways that allow for adaptability such that we move towards a future built for all humans.

## Principle 6: Placing importance on the material consequences of the work we do
THiCC Lab mixes theory with placing a high importance on the material consequences of the work we are doing, including the lived impacts of the technological theories and systems we develop and use.
In the lab, we towards structures and support that acknowledge the work of our project members through equitable compensation and attribution.

## Principle 7: Critically creating technological tools for Black communities
Throughout all of our work we are sure to be critical in the technological tools we create, particularly with a mind for how existing engineering processes may lead to the othering of Black folks and Black communities. We also look towards the future and, in that envisioning, leave space for the [radical dreams, joy, and persistance](https://www.nationalequityproject.org/blog/freedom-dreaming) needed for futures beyond [Man](https://globalsocialtheory.org/concepts/the-human/).
