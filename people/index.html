ThiCC lab members

Director of THiCC Lab

         

Dr. Chris Dancy is an Associate Professor of Industrial Engineering, Computer Science & Engineering, and African American Studies, and is Director of THiCC lab, co-Director of the Center for Black Digital Research/#DgBlk, founder and director of the Liberatory Tech Project, and the co-lead of the Computational Representation of Human Decision Processes research thrust in the NSF AI Institute for Societal Decision Making. His interest and expertise lies at the intersections of human behavior, computational systems, and sociocultural structures. He uses theoretical perspectives from computational cognitive science and Black studies to ground his research at these intersections. To study the processes that define these interactions, Dr. Dancy uses computational cognitive modeling, human behavioral studies, and design & implementation considerations of computational (particularly “AI”) systems.

Graduate Students

Deja Workman is a PhD student in Information Sciences and Technology at Penn State and a graduate research assistant working in THiCC Lab as well as the Liberatory Tech Project as a part of the Center for Black Digtial Research/#DigBlk. In 2022 she graduated from Penn State with a B.S. in information sciences and technology. Her current research project focuses on anti-blackness in the artificial intelligence development process, and her past research projects include testing learning theories on complex tasks as well as diversity in STEM education and inclusive instructor behaviors.

Meera Ray is a PhD student in Computer Science and Engineering at Penn State and a graduate research assistant in THiCC Lab. She graduated with a B.S. in Statistics and Machine Learning from Carnegie Mellon in 2023. Her research aims to articulate how artificial intelligence interacts with social hierarchies. Meera’s current research project, developing a cognitive model of human decision making in disaster situations, is with the AI Institute for Societal Decision Making (AI-SDM).

Swapnika Dulam is a PhD student in Computer Science at Penn State and a Graduate Research Assistant in THiCC Lab. She is from India and did her Integrated Dual Degree (B.Tech + M.Tech (CSE)) in JNTUHCEH, Hyderabad, India. She has four years of industry experience as a full stack developer and also designed distributed systems using Restful API. Her Current research project focuses on Human AI Interaction where she is working on Minecraft malmo environment.

Emmanuel Adjei Domfeh is a first-year Ph.D. student in Computer Science and Engineering at Penn State University and a Graduate Research Assistant at the THICC Lab. He holds a Bachelor of Science and a Master of Philosophy in Computer Science from the University of Energy and Natural Resources, Ghana, where he previously served as a Teaching and Research Assistant and later as an Assistant Lecturer. Emmanuel’s research is rooted in Human-Centered AI, with a focus on designing Reliable, Safe, and Trustworthy AI systems. His current work centers on projects working with the AI Institute of Social Decision Making (AI-SDM), which explores Human-AI use patterns in disaster recovery. The objectives of his research include: 1.Understanding the diverse Human-AI use patterns in disaster recovery scenarios. 2.Developing computational decision models to optimize interactions between humans and AI during critical decision-making processes. 3.Creating virtual environments and simulations to illustrate and analyze these use patterns in disaster scenarios.

Fitsum Beyene is a PhD student in Computer Science and Engineering currently working in THiCC Lab and with the Liberatory Tech Project as a part of the Center for Black Digtial Research/#DigBlk

Julian Gay is a PhD student in Computer Science and Engineering at Penn State and a graduate research assistant in the THICC Lab. He graduated with a B.S. in Computer Science from the University of Texas at Dallas. His research aims to understand the racialization of AI systems through building cognitive agents and virtual environments. His past research and expertise involved incorporating artificial intelligence in industry as a software developer and engineer using neural networks and game theory.
